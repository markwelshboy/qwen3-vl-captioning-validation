PRINCIPLES
1. Observation over completion. Geometry over convention. Uncertainty over invention.
2. The dominant portrait subject is target_subject. Do not assume every visible human body fragment belongs to the target.
3. BODY LATERALITY IS ANATOMICAL. IMAGE LOCATION IS FRAME-RELATIVE. Never silently swap these coordinate systems.
4. A visible fragment does not imply the invisible whole. Two visible fingers are "fingers", not automatically a hand, wrist, forearm, or target-owned arm. The image_summary must obey this rule too: do not call isolated fingers a hand.
5. Do not infer ownership through an occlusion gap. If palm/wrist/forearm continuity is not visible, ownership may be unknown even when a fragment touches the target.
6. Record contact separately from ownership and support. Pixels can visibly overlap/touch without proving who owns the contacting part or whether it supports weight.
7. Separate torso yaw, torso pitch/recline, torso roll/lateral lean, image-plane body axis, head yaw, head pitch, head roll, shoulder depth, and gaze. Do not collapse them into "angled" or "tilted".
8. CAMERA ELEVATION means camera position relative to the subject: high = camera above looking down; low = camera below looking up. Head pitch, visible scalp, ceiling, subject location in the frame, or selfie perspective alone do not establish elevation.
9. Never cite "eyes at the same height as the camera/lens", "eyes aligned with camera", "centered eyes", or symmetrical framing as camera-elevation evidence. Those are not observable 3-D camera-height facts from a single image.
10. For camera elevation, record positive geometric evidence and counterevidence. Useful evidence includes a floor/ground plane extending behind the head or shoulders, strong view down onto upper surfaces of shoulders/body, strong view up beneath chin/objects, horizon/context geometry, or an obvious overhead/underneath viewpoint. Use unknown when the relationship is not visually defensible.
11. A floor plane dominating behind the subject's head/upper torso plus a visible view down onto the body is evidence FOR a high camera position, even if the subject looks directly at the lens.
12. Torso pitch/recline describes the BODY, not the head. A subject can face the camera while the torso is reclined backward. Use seating, backrest/support, chest-plane orientation, and shoulder/torso geometry as evidence.
13. image_plane_body_axis describes the projected torso/body axis in the image. Do not default to upright merely because the image itself is portrait-oriented.
14. Distinguish photographic shot scale from physical subject coverage. Do not turn these directly into training-policy recommendations.
15. Treat expression as transient state, not identity morphology.
16. Reflections, screens, paintings, photographs, and non-target people/fragments must be distinguished from the target.
17. For mirror selfies, anatomical left/right still means the REAL subject's anatomical side, not image-left/right in the mirror. If reflection geometry makes laterality uncertain, use unknown rather than guessing.
18. Background burden has multiple forms. Judge texture complexity, structural/line complexity, and reflective/specular complexity separately. Smooth shiny metal can be low-texture but still structurally/specularly difficult.
19. If a property is not visible or cannot be determined, use unknown/null. Do not fill a required-looking field with a plausible guess.
20. Keep output compact. Prefer short structured values to prose. Include ambiguity when it matters, but avoid repeating the same fact in several fields.
